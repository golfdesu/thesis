# Summary: Hierarchical Probabilistic Conformal Prediction for Distributed Energy Resources Adoption

## 🎯 Main Objective & Contribution
- Predict **DER adoption** (rooftop solar PV installations) with **uncertainty quantification that remains statistically valid at both circuit and substation levels** of the distribution-grid hierarchy — marginal circuit-level intervals do not guarantee valid aggregated substation coverage (shown via counterexample where Y₁+Y₂ ≡ 0 escapes [2,4]).
- Proposes **[[HPCP]]** (Hierarchical Probabilistic Conformal Prediction): a [[Multivariate_Hawkes_Process]] base predictor + tailored split [[Conformal_Prediction]] with a **topology-aware non-conformity score** using "sibling circuits".
- Contributions: (i) distribution-free framework satisfying both coverage constraints (Eqs. 1–2); (ii) theoretical finite-sample validity under strong-mixing residuals and universal efficiency guarantees (Prop. 1: sharper than PCP-style and any ℓp-norm score); (iii) superior empirical accuracy & calibration on synthetic + real Indianapolis data.

## 🧠 Methodology & Model Architecture
### Problem setup
K circuits, R substations, bipartite **affiliation matrix** $C \in \{0,1\}^{K\times R}$ ($c_{k,r}=1$ ⇔ circuit k under substation r). Target: intervals $\hat{L}, \hat{U}$ such that (**Eq. 1 circuit / Eq. 2 substation level**):
$$P\left(\hat{L}_k \leq Y_{n+1,k} \leq \hat{U}_k\right) \geq 1-\alpha, \quad P\left([C^\top\hat{L}]_r \leq [C^\top Y_{n+1}]_r \leq [C^\top\hat{U}]_r\right) \geq 1-\alpha$$

### Multivariate Hawkes process base model
Conditional intensity definition (**Eq. 3**): $\lambda_k(t|H_t) = \lim_{\delta\to0}\frac{\mathbb{E}[N_k(t+\delta)-N_k(t)|H_t]}{\delta}$. Log-likelihood for MLE fitting (**Eq. 4**):
$$\ell(H_T) = \sum_{k=1}^K \left(\int_0^T \log\lambda_k(t|H_t)\,dN_k(t) - \int_0^T \lambda_k(t|H_t)\,dt\right)$$
Parameterized self/mutually-exciting intensity with exponential kernel and saturation factor (**Eq. 5 — Multivariate Hawkes intensity**):
$$\lambda_k(t|\mathcal{H}_t,\mu) = \gamma(t)\left[\mu_k(t) + \sum_{t'<t}\sum_{k'=1}^K \alpha_{k,k'} \cdot \kappa(t,t')\right], \quad \kappa(t,t') := \beta e^{-\beta(t-t')}$$
where $A = (\alpha_{k,k'})$ is the circuit-interaction matrix, $\mu_k(t)$ an exogenous adoption rate (linear in socioeconomic/policy covariates), $\gamma(t)$ models saturation toward adoption ceiling. Forecasts generated by **Ogata thinning-sampling**: M simulated trajectories → counts per window (**Eq. 6**): $\tilde{y}_{j,k} := \tilde{N}_k([t_{j-1}, t_j))$.

### Hierarchical conformal calibration
Sibling matrix $S := CC^\top \in \{0,1\}^{K\times K}$ ($s_{k,k'}=1$ ⇔ same substation). Topology-aware non-conformity score over sibling circuits only (**Eq. 7 — Hierarchical non-conformity score**):
$$e_{j,k} = \min_{1 \le m \le M} \left\| \mathbf{S}_k \odot (y_j - \tilde{y}_j^{(m)}) \right\|_\infty$$
Prediction intervals from empirical α-quantile $\hat{Q}_k(\hat{\alpha})$ of calibration scores, taking extremal endpoints across M simulations (**Eq. 8**):
$$\hat{L}_k = \min_{1\le m\le M}\tilde{y}_{n+1,k}^{(m)} - \hat{Q}_k(\hat{\alpha}), \qquad \hat{U}_k = \max_{1\le m\le M}\tilde{y}_{n+1,k}^{(m)} + \hat{Q}_k(\hat{\alpha})$$

### Theory
- **Theorem 5.1 (Validity)** under strong-mixing residuals: coverage (1)–(2) holds if nominal level set as (**Eq. 9**):
$$\hat{\alpha} \leq \left[1 - \frac{1-\alpha}{1 - ((\log n)^2 W/2n)^{1/3} - (W/2)^{1/3}(\log n)^{2/3}/n^{1/3}}\right]_+$$
(W = mixing bound; setting $\hat\alpha=\alpha$ yields asymptotic validity.)
- **Proposition 1 (Efficiency)**: $V_{HPCP} \leq V_{PCP}$ and $V_{HPCP} \leq V_p\;\forall p$, where efficiency $V = \frac{1}{K}\sum_k \mathbb{E}[\hat{U}_k - \hat{L}_k]$.
- Weighted quantile adjustment by temporal proximity handles distribution shift ([[2021_Stankeviciute_Conformal_Time_Series_Forecasting]]-adjacent line of work).

## 📊 Dataset & Input Features
- **[[Indianapolis_DER_Adoption_Dataset]]** — customer-level rooftop solar panel installation records from Indianapolis, Indiana, provided by the local utility (**Indianapolis Power & Light Co. dba AES Indiana**, which funded the project). Spanning **2010–2024**, containing **1,742 installation records** across **K = 245 circuits** and **R = 51 substations**. Each record includes address, application date, and circuit/substation connectivity IDs. No public URL (utility-private).
- Augmenting covariates (utility + U.S. Census Bureau https://data.census.gov , accessed 2025-04-15): average power outages (**SAIDI** = total outage minutes / number of customers, utility data 2004–Aug 2024 with exact coordinates/durations); feeder-level average electrical load in kVA (sum of three-phase currents × operating voltage ÷ √3, 2021–2023); mean electricity price; average education level; median household income (2022 inflation-adjusted, census-tract level); plus total annual average rooftop solar generation potential from **Google Project Sunroof** (August 2019 release).
- Synthetic data: intercorrelated Poisson variables via Gaussian copula over a VAR(1) process, varying sparsity (#substations), Poisson intensity, spatial/temporal rank correlation.
- Rolling out-of-sample evaluation (fit on all past windows, predict next window, default Δt = 1 month); Hawkes fitted with Adam optimizer, lr = 1e−2, ≤1000 epochs.

## 📈 Performance & Results
- **Point-prediction MAE (Table 1, rolling 2019–2023, mean ± std over 100 trials)**: Multivariate Hawkes lowest in 9 of 12 resolutions: monthly **0.06±0.02**, seasonal (3-mo) 0.21±0.08, 5-mo **0.41±0.19**, vs Discrete Hawkes (0.08/0.24/0.42), VAR (0.12/0.29/0.45), GP (0.12/0.30/0.47), LSTM (0.16/0.37/0.54), RNN (0.20/0.38/0.55). For 9–12-month horizons Discrete Hawkes wins slightly (annual 0.91 vs 0.97). ML baselines fail due to sparse, stochastic event data.
- **Uncertainty quantification (Fig. 7, nominal 90%)**: HPCP closest to bottom-right ideal at BOTH circuit and substation levels; VAR/GP/QR baselines either miss coverage (< reference line) or produce overly wide intervals. Marginal conformal fails substation coverage; HPCP matches Joint/Bonferroni coverage with significantly smaller interval sizes.
- **Synthetic ablations**: HPCP keeps coverage above nominal across sparsity/intensity/spatial-correlation settings while shrinking interval size with sparser topologies and higher spatial correlation; robust to temporal dependence.
- **Sensitivity**: interval size convex in simulation size M with minimum near **M = 5** (contrasts with monotonically-improving PCP); coverage stable and exceeds target as $\hat\alpha$ varies; interval size grows ~linearly as $\hat\alpha \to 0$.
- **One-year-ahead spatial forecast (2025–2026)**: northern/southwestern Indianapolis regions show highest projected adoption and highest uncertainty — compounded planning risk visualization.

## 💡 Limitations & Identified Research Gaps
- Assumes a **fixed two-level hierarchy with linear (summation) aggregation**; deeper hierarchies (pole–circuit–substation–region) and nonlinear aggregations (e.g., sigmoid voltage-stability impacts) unsupported.
- Primarily suited to short-to-medium horizon forecasts; data-driven nature cannot anticipate events absent from history (e.g., future DER policy shifts) — long-horizon planning remains hard.
- Requires known, static topology C; restrictive for dynamic/unknown networks (healthcare, economics, social analysis).
- Authors' future work: non-conformity scores compatible with estimated/non-static hierarchies; richer network structures; longer-term forecasting tasks.
