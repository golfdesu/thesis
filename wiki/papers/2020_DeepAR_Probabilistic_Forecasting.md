# Summary: DeepAR: Probabilistic Forecasting with Autoregressive Recurrent Networks

## 🎯 Main Objective & Contribution
- Introduces **[[DeepAR]]**, a methodology for probabilistic forecasting based on training an **autoregressive recurrent network** (multi-layer [[LSTM]]) as a **global model** across a large number of related time series.
- Key contributions:
	1. An RNN architecture for probabilistic forecasting incorporating a **negative-binomial likelihood** for count data plus special treatment for **power-law scale heterogeneity** across series (item-dependent rescaling + velocity-weighted sampling).
	2. Empirical demonstration of ~**15% accuracy improvement** over state-of-the-art methods on several real-world datasets.
- Advantages over classical per-series methods: minimal manual feature engineering; Monte Carlo samples yield consistent quantile estimates for all sub-ranges; forecasts for items with **little or no history**; flexible likelihood choice (no Gaussian-noise assumption).

## 🧠 Methodology & Model Architecture
- Goal: model the conditional distribution $P(z_{i,t_0:T} \mid z_{i,1:t_0-1}, x_{i,1:T})$ where $z_{i,t}$ is the value of series $i$ at time $t$, $[1, t_0-1]$ is the **conditioning range**, and $[t_0, T]$ the **prediction range**; $x_{i,1:T}$ are covariates known for all time points.
- The model distribution factorizes into a product of likelihoods parametrized by the autoregressive RNN output (**Autoregressive Recurrence Equation**):
$$Q_\Theta(z_{i,t_0:T} \mid z_{i,1:t_0-1}, x_{i,1:T}) = \prod_{t=t_0}^{T} Q_\Theta(z_{i,t} \mid z_{i,1:t-1}, x_{i,1:T}) = \prod_{t=t_0}^{T} \ell\big(z_{i,t} \mid \theta(h_{i,t}, \Theta)\big)$$

$$h_{i,t} = h\,(h_{i,t-1}, z_{i,t-1}, x_{i,t}, \Theta) \tag{1}$$
where $h$ is a multi-layer RNN with [[LSTM]] cells (3 layers); encoder and decoder share weights (same architecture for conditioning and prediction ranges). Initial state $h_{i,0}$ and $z_{i,0}$ are zero-initialized.
- **Prediction**: ancestral sampling — draw $\tilde{z}_{i,t} \sim \ell(\cdot \mid \theta(\tilde{h}_{i,t}, \Theta))$ for $t \geq t_0$ and feed back until $t_0+T$; repeating yields sample traces representing the joint predicted distribution (200 decoder samples used).
- **Gaussian likelihood (for real-valued data)**:
$$\ell_G(z \mid \mu, \sigma) = (2\pi\sigma^2)^{-\frac{1}{2}} \exp\!\big(-(z-\mu)^2 / (2\sigma^2)\big)$$
$$\mu(h_{i,t}) = w_\mu^\top h_{i,t} + b_\mu \quad \text{and} \quad \sigma(h_{i,t}) = \log(1 + \exp(w_\sigma^\top h_{i,t} + b_\sigma))$$
(softplus ensures $\sigma > 0$).
- **Negative-binomial likelihood (for positive count data)**, parametrized by mean $\mu \in \mathbb{R}_+$ and shape $\alpha \in \mathbb{R}_+$:
$$\ell_{NB}(z \mid \mu, \alpha) = \frac{\Gamma(z + \frac{1}{\alpha})}{\Gamma(z+1)\,\Gamma(\frac{1}{\alpha})}\left(\frac{1}{1+\alpha\mu}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}} \left(\frac{\alpha\mu}{1+\alpha\mu}\right)^{z}$$
$$\mu(h_{i,t}) = \log(1 + \exp(w_\mu^\top h_{i,t} + b_\mu)) \quad \text{and} \quad \alpha(h_{i,t}) = \log(1 + \exp(w_\alpha^\top h_{i,t} + b_\alpha))$$
with $\mathrm{Var}[z] = \mu + \mu^2 \alpha$; both parameters via fully-connected layers with softplus activation (conducive to fast convergence).
- **Training objective (Log-Likelihood Maximization)**:
$$\mathcal{L} = \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{t=t_0}^{T} \log \ell\big(z_{i,t} \mid \theta(h_{i,t})\big) \tag{2}$$
optimized directly via stochastic gradient descent ([[Adam]] optimizer, early stopping) — no latent-variable inference needed unlike state space models. Training instances are generated by sliding windows with different start points; windows may start before the series' first observation with zero padding so the model learns behavior of "new" series. Scheduled sampling was tried but gave no significant accuracy gain.
- **Scale handling**: autoregressive inputs divided by an item-dependent scale $\nu_i$, scale-dependent likelihood parameters multiplied by it: for negative binomial, $\mu = \nu_i \log(1+\exp(o_\mu))$, $\alpha = \log(1+\exp(o_\alpha))/\sqrt{\nu_i}$, with heuristic $\nu_i = 1 + \frac{1}{t_0}\sum_{t=1}^{t_0} z_{i,t}$ (**Average-Scale Heuristic**). Additionally, **velocity-weighted sampling**: probability of selecting a window from an example with scale $\nu_i$ proportional to $\nu_i$, compensating the power-law skew of item velocities.
- Implementation in MXNet on a single AWS p2.xlarge (4 CPUs + 1 GPU); full run on the 500K-series `ec` dataset < 10 h. Hyperparameters grid-searched (item output embedding dim, # LSTM nodes) minimizing validation NLL.

## 📊 Dataset & Input Features
| Dataset | # Series | Granularity | Encoder/Decoder length |
|---|---|---|---|
| parts (US automobile monthly sales) | 1046 | month | 8 / 8 |
| electricity (370 customers' consumption) | 370 | hourly | 168 / 24 |
| traffic (SF bay area freeway lane occupancy) | 963 | hourly | 168 / 24 |
| ec-sub (Amazon weekly item sales) | 39,700 | week | 52 / 52 |
| ec (Amazon weekly item sales) | 534,884 | week | 52 / 52 |

- Splits: parts → first 42 months train, last 8 test; electricity → train 2014-01-01 to 2014-09-01; traffic → all data before 2008-06-15; ec/ec-sub → forecast start 2014-09-07, predict 52 weeks. Electricity/traffic evaluated via rolling-window predictions (single trained model, no retraining per window).
- No public URLs given in the text; parts/ec/ec-sub are non-public Amazon/company data. electricity and traffic originate from Yu et al. (temporal regularized matrix factorization, NeurIPS 2016).
- Features (covariates $x_{i,t}$): **age** feature (distance to first observation of the series), day-of-week and hour-of-day (hourly data), week-of-year (weekly), month-of-year (monthly) encoded as increasing numeric values; a single categorical **item feature** learned as an embedding (product category for retail, item identity for small datasets); all covariates standardized to zero mean/unit variance.
- Missing observations handled by replacing unobserved $z_{i,t}$ with samples from the conditional predictive distribution and excluding those terms from the likelihood.

## 📈 Performance & Results
- Metrics: **ρ-risk** (normalized quantile loss, $\rho \in \{0.5, 0.9\}$) over spans $[L, L+S)$, ND (normalized deviation), RMSE. Quantile loss definition: $\mathcal{L}_\rho(Z, \hat{Z}^\rho) = 2(\hat{Z}-Z)\big(\rho\,\mathbb{I}_{\hat{Z}^\rho > Z} - (1-\rho)\,\mathbb{I}_{\hat{Z}^\rho \leq Z}\big)$; ρ-risk = $\big(\sum_i \mathcal{L}_\rho(Z_i, \hat{Z}_i^\rho)\big)/\big(\sum_i Z_i\big)$. ND = $\frac{\sum_{i,t}|z_{i,t}-\hat{z}_{i,t}|}{\sum_{i,t}|z_{i,t}|}$.
- Count-data demand sets (relative risk vs strongest published baseline; lower better):
	- parts (baseline Snyder): DeepAR avg 0.5-risk **0.94**, 0.9-risk **0.94** vs ISSM 1.08/1.01, ETS 1.23/1.08, rnn-negbin 0.99/0.99.
	- ec-sub (baseline ISSM): DeepAR **0.77** avg (0.5-risk all spans) and **0.57** (0.9-risk) — large gains vs ETS 1.07, rnn-gaussian 1.21.
	- ec (baseline ISSM): DeepAR **0.85** / **0.91** average vs rnn-negbin 0.93/0.98, Croston 1.34.
- Point forecasting (vs matrix factorization MatFact):
	- electricity: ND **0.07** vs 0.16; NRMSE **1.00** vs 1.15.
	- traffic: ND **0.17** vs 0.20; NRMSE **0.42** vs 0.43.
- Ablations: rnn-gaussian (Gaussian likelihood) clearly worse → count distributions matter; rnn-negbin without scaling/weighted sampling worse on power-law sets (ec, ec-sub) but similar on parts.
- Calibration: Coverage(p) closer to diagonal than ISSM; shuffling samples (destroying temporal correlation) worsens calibration and raises 0.9-risk by ~10% on long spans → joint sample paths capture inter-temporal correlation. Learned uncertainty growth is non-linear (higher in Q4) vs ISSM's imposed linear growth.

## 💡 Limitations & Identified Research Gaps
- Autoregressive teacher-forcing mismatch between training and prediction (exposure bias); scheduled sampling did not help here but remains a known issue in other domains.
- Scale factor choice ($\nu_i$) is a heuristic; challenging with missing data or large within-series variance.
- Marginal independence across time steps in some settings; correlations only via sampled paths.
- Hyper-parameter tuning could overfit the validation split; authors suggest non-overlapping time intervals for tuning.
- Evaluation with missing prediction-range data requires adjusted metrics not comparable across studies (not addressed experimentally).
